ProcureGuard: From Data Exceptions to Verified Resolution

How can a team turn unreliable procurement records into traceable issues, document corrective actions, and confirm that a fix actually works?

Executive summary

I designed and built ProcureGuard as an end-to-end, reproducible synthetic procurement data-quality and governance demonstration. It combines an offline rule engine, a persistent issue register with investigation and ownership, a fresh-data verification gate, seven SQL reporting views and a three-page Power BI dashboard.

The demonstration deliberately starts with flawed fictional data, records a failed verification before the source is fixed, then rechecks the corrected source and verifies resolution of one case.

What I built and why

  • Explicit quality checks: Six configurable rules covering missing and duplicate identifiers, broken relational references, potential supplier duplicates, questionable contract values, and invalid dates or currency-code formats.
  • Stateful investigation: SQLite retains stable row identities, issue assignments, investigation notes, corrective actions, statuses and actor-attributed audit events.
  • Verification before closure: A case is resolved only when its issue no longer appears during a fresh validation of the authorized source record.
  • Reporting layer: Seven SQL views export reconciled CSVs for case KPIs, rule summaries, owner workloads, scan comparisons, activity and verification evidence.
  • Analyst-facing interface: Power BI connects the technical controls to clear operational questions about outstanding issues and verification outcomes.
Design choice: missing or nominal reported contract values and normalized supplier-name matches are review flags, not automatic evidence of financial error, fraud, or legal-entity duplication.

Power BI dashboard: three perspectives

1. Quality Overview

Current case KPIs, issues by rule, status composition, and filters for dataset, severity and status.

2. Issue Management

Unresolved and unassigned workload, high-priority issues, responsible owner breakdown, and a detailed exception register for investigation.

3. Quality Trends & Performance

Two-scan comparison of row-level data quality, detected issues, the failed and successful verification events, and a concise interpretation of the change.

Results and interpretation

The initial synthetic scan detected 22 issues and a 42.11% row-level pass rate. Following the scripted correction, the next scan detected 21 issues and a 44.74% pass rate: a difference of 2.63 percentage points. The current case register records 21 unresolved cases, including 14 high-priority unresolved, and one resolved case.

One verification fails against unchanged source data before a corrected source record passes a new check. The point is to illustrate control design and evidence-backed closure, not to imply that a real organization achieved measurable gains.

Scope: all 38 source rows, entities, transactions and verification events are fictional. Only two sequential demo scans exist; this is not a sustained time series or production deployment. Actors are illustrative text labels, not authenticated users. No real procurement or financial impact is claimed.
