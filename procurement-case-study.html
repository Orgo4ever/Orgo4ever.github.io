UK Pharmaceutical Procurement Analytics

How can procurement teams report useful supplier and award insights when their contract-value data is too incomplete or ambiguous to support trustworthy spending analysis?

MySQLPower QueryDAXTableauData validation

Executive summary

I analyzed a supplied UK pharmaceutical procurement dataset and built interactive reporting around procurement activity, award concentration, supplier participation and data reliability. A central decision was not to calculate a misleading expenditure total from nominal and missing contract values.

In this dataset, 12 procurement processes accounted for 69.56% of recorded awards. This measures award-count concentration, not spending concentration.

My contribution and analytical approach

  • Worked with the supplied database structure and developed MySQL analytical queries using joins, aggregation, CTEs, views and window functions.
  • Prepared four reporting tables using Power Query, retained identifiers as text, normalized null representations and preserved nominal £1 values for quality reporting.
  • Built Power BI measures and relationships that preserve differences between procurement, award, supplier association and contract granularity.
  • Developed Power BI and Tableau views of procedures, supplier associations, buyer activity and contract-value usability.
  • Reconciled Power BI totals against SQL outputs and checked procurement-method filters to test reporting consistency.

The data-quality finding

Across 1,216 contract records, 767 had a nominal £1 value and 24 had no reported value; together these account for 65.05%. The records may reflect reporting conventions rather than simple entry errors. Without further source verification, they should not be interpreted as true expenditure.

Reporting decision: emphasize verified activity counts, award concentration and supplier participation, while documenting that spending comparisons are unreliable.

Missing and nominal values are quality flags for analytical use, not automatically proven source-data mistakes. The analysis does not establish procurement savings, production deployment or a completed remediation programme.

How this work could be operationalized

A logical next step is to move from identifying quality problems to maintaining a rules catalogue, an exceptions register, investigation owners and repeatable reconciliation tests. That would support data stewardship and ongoing procurement reporting rather than a one-time analytical view.

This is a proposed extension, not a claim that the operational governance workflow is already implemented.

Access and transparency

The Tableau dashboard and screenshots are publicly viewable. The source-code repository and Power BI desktop file are currently private; they are not presented as public downloads.
